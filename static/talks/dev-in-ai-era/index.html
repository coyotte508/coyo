The baseline

How it used to work

Knowledge = years

Fluency in a stack came from years of grinding docs, source code and Stack Overflow. Domain gaps were expensive.

Cross-cutting work = grind

Translating an app into several languages, adapting to a specific UI/UX: pure mechanical weeks nobody enjoyed.

Big refactors = risk

Moving large pieces of code around was slow, scary, and gated on one person holding it all in their head.

Side tools = never

Internal tools and one-off projects lost the prioritization fight every single time.

Where it shines

What well-prompted AI is great at

  • Filling gaps in your domain knowledge — new languages, unfamiliar stacks, foreign codebases
  • Translating & adapting — i18n, porting apps, matching a specific UI style or design system
  • Refactoring large chunks of code — context no human can hold
  • One-off, non-core projects — internal tools, prototypes, glue
Well-prompted AI turns weeks of grind into hours of review.
Eliott C. — Development in the AI era06
What unlocked it

The new toolkit — and why now

👀 Vision

Screenshot → fix. Mock → app. The UI, long the 'hard to specify' part, became directly addressable.

🔧 Tool use

Agents that run commands, read & write files, test their work, self-correct. Suggestion → action.

🧩 Compounding basics

Long context, structured outputs, sub-agents, skills. Small capabilities that add up.

🏋️ Recommended exercise: build your own harness

A thin layer that calls the model — your own tool loop, your own mini coding-agent. You learn more about the real capabilities & limits than from any polished product.

Eliott C. — Development in the AI era07
Where you stay essential

For your core, keep the handle

🏢 Deep products ≠ one-offs

The main project — where you collaborate, that outlives your attention — must stay tidy: architecture, tests, conventions, CI, docs.

AI can generate infinite code.
It can't generate a codebase you still love in 6 months — you steer that.

🧭 Judgment > intelligence

AI is biased by your questions — ask "how do I X with Y" and it will never say "you shouldn't X".

Zoom out regularly: other solutions? a pivot?

Even the most powerful AI (the fables of today) doesn't always have the best judgment.
